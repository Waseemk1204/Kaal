# KAAL — Story Bible

> **Kaal**: time. Also: death. In the old stories they are the same thing.
>
> *As long as the lamp burns, Kaal waits.*

Jam theme: **Everything is Temporary.**
Genre: first-person atmospheric horror. No jump scares. A slow walk toward
something that cannot be stopped, only delayed.

---

## 1. The premise in one breath

A Sunday night in 1987. A family of four eats dinner in an old house in a
Lucknow galli. Dadi tells a story about Kaal, the faceless thing that is time.
The power goes. The lights flicker, and with every flicker someone is missing.
In the dark, you strike a match — and the house you are standing in is the
same house forty years later: rotted, rooted through, condemned, empty.

Light brings back *then* for as long as it burns. Darkness is *now*. And in the
dark, something very tall is walking toward you, and it has never once had to
hurry.

## 2. What is really going on (never stated outright)

You are not the child at the dinner. You are **Munna, seventy years old**, the
last of the family. The house is being demolished in the morning. You came back
to sit at the table one last time, with a box of matches because the power was
cut years ago. Dadi died in '91, Papa in '04, Mummy in '19. You can no longer
remember any of their faces.

Tonight is the night Kaal comes for the last thing left in the house.

Whether Kaal is real, or Dadi's bedtime story wearing the shape of an old man's
death, the game never says. Both readings must hold.

### Why nobody has a face
Dadi's rule: *"First the face, then the name, then the person."* Kaal has already taken the faces. Munna remembers his
family's hands, voices, clothes, habits — but every head is a smooth, blank
oval. This is the single most important visual rule in the game: **no character
ever has a face, and the story explains why.**

## 3. Characters

None of them have facial features. They are recognised by silhouette, clothes,
posture, hands, objects and voice.

| Who | Silhouette & look | Habit (how you know them) | Their anchor object |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Munna** (you) | First person. In the intro, a child's eye height (1.1 m). In play, an adult's (1.65 m) — nobody tells you why. | Your hands: in match light they look young. In moonlight they are large, spotted, trembling. | — |
| **Mummy** | Maroon cotton saree, pallu over shoulder, hair in a long plait. Always half-turned toward the kitchen. | Red glass bangles. You hear her before you see her. | **Her red glass bangles** |
| **Papa** | White half-sleeve shirt, grey trousers, side-parted hair, reading glasses pushed up on his head. Leans back in his chair. | Checks his HMT wristwatch whenever he's bored or worried. | **His HMT wristwatch** (stopped at 9:47) |
| **Dadi** | White saree, small and bent, white hair in a bun, a shawl even in summer. | Tells the same stories. Reads the Gita with her spectacles. | **Her spectacles** |

### KAAL

**What it is.** Time, given a body. The thing that took Dadi's face, then Papa's,
then Mummy's. It does not hate you. It does not want anything. It simply
arrives, the way evening arrives.

**What it looks like.**
- **Too tall for the house.** 2.8 m. The ceilings are 3.2 m and the doorways 2.1 m,
  so it is always stooped — and at every doorway it has to *fold* itself
  through: head first, then the shoulders narrowing, then the long arms dragging
  in after.
- **Lanky past the point of being human.** A narrow torso, a neck slightly too
  long, a small smooth egg of a head tipped forward as if listening. Arms that
  hang past its knees. Fingers a hand-span long, with one joint too many.
  Shoulder blades and spine-knobs push through the silhouette when it bends.
- **Black like a hole.** Not dark grey, not shiny. Light falls on it and nothing
  comes back. Even in a lit room it reads as a cut-out in the picture.
- **Burning at the edges.** Fine flakes of ash lift off its shoulders and drift
  upward, like the edge of a burning page. When time stops in your light, the
  ash stops too — hanging in the air around it.
- **No face. No mouth. No eyes.** Nothing. The head is smooth all the way round.

**How it moves.**
- **It never runs.** Ever. It walks slowly, and it pauses — mid-step, for long,
  long seconds — and then continues the step exactly where it left off.
- **It moves one joint at a time.** The knee, then the hip, then the shoulder.
  Like something remembering how bodies work.
- **It touches the walls.** Fingertips drag along the plaster as it walks,
  and where it touches, the wall ages — paint blisters, damp blooms, plaster
  falls. You can read where it has been.
- **In your light, it stops.** Completely. Except the head. The head keeps
  turning, slowly, to follow you.

**How it sounds.**
- **Ticking.** Its only voice: dozens of tiny clock-ticks, out of step with each
  other, like a drawer full of old wristwatches. Quiet and far, then near.
- **No footsteps.** It is silent where it walks. You hear it by what is missing.
- **The house goes quiet around it.** Crickets, wind, the creak of beams — all
  of it drops away when Kaal is close. Silence is the warning.
- **Knuckles.** A slow run of cracks, one finger at a time, when it starts
  moving again after being held.
- **Plaster.** A long dry scrape: fingertips on a wall, in the next room.

**What it does to things.** Whatever it touches, it ages. Near it, your match
burns down faster, a candle melts faster, a fresh roti goes stale in your hand.
When it touches *you*, you get forty years in four seconds.

## 4. The house

One storey, built 1958, in a galli in old Lucknow. Three rooms in a row behind
a barred front door. A small courtyard out back that only Kaal walks in.

| Room | **Tab** (then — match light) | **Ab** (now — moonlight) |
|---|---|---|
| **Dining room** | Warm. Steel plates, rajma-chawal steaming, the Weston TV, the wall clock at 9:47, the family photo, a brass diya on the table. | Part of the ceiling has fallen in; a shaft of moonlight lands on the table. Rusted plates still set. The TV face-down under roots. The photo above the sideboard: four blank-faced figures. A red stencil through the front window jaali: *DANGEROUS STRUCTURE — TO BE DEMOLISHED.* |
| **Kitchen** | Mummy's kitchen mid-dinner: atta on the board, the pressure cooker, the steel shelves, the jaali window to the courtyard. | Half the floor has caved into the old underground water tank — a black pit. Rusted cooker on a dead stove. The window jaali looks out onto the moonlit courtyard. |
| **Dadi's room** | Her charpai, her tin trunk, the pooja shelf with the brass thali and the Gita, a 1987 calendar. | A peepal tree has grown up through the floor and out the roof. Roots everywhere. And on every wall, hung from every root: **clocks**. Wall clocks, alarm clocks, wristwatches on nails. Dozens. All ticking, none agreeing. This is where Kaal rests. |

## 5. The story, beat by beat

### Prologue — *Ravivar, 1987. 9:44 PM.* (interactive cutscene, ~2 min)
Child's eye height. The ceiling fan turns. The TV mutters the news. Steel on
steel. Mummy serves; her bangles chime with every spoonful.

Dadi, to you, while Papa rolls his eyes:

> **DADI:** Do you know what Kaal looks like, Munna?
>
> **DADI:** So tall it has to bow at every door.
>
> **DADI:** It has no face. Faces are the first thing it takes. First the face,
> then the name, then the person.
>
> **PAPA:** Amma, don't scare the boy.
>
> **DADI:** It never runs. Why would it hurry? Everyone comes to it in the end.
>
> **DADI:** Only one thing stops it. As long as the lamp burns, Kaal waits.

(Everything Dadi says is a rule of the game. The player has just been taught
how to survive without a single tutorial box.)

The fan slows. The tube light stutters.

> **PAPA:** There goes the power again.
>
> **MUMMY:** Munna, get the matches. On the sideboard.

**Control is given to you** — still at child height — to walk to the sideboard
and pick up the matchbox. Behind you, the tube light flickers. Each flicker,
something at the table has changed, and you only see it if you turn around:

1. Flicker — Dadi's chair is empty. Her spectacles are on her plate.
2. Flicker — the wall clock has stopped. 9:47.
3. Flicker — Papa is gone. His watch lies by his plate. The TV is snow.
4. Flicker — Mummy is gone. Her bangles are not on the table. They're nowhere.
5. Black. Silence. Not even the fan.

Prompt: **strike a match.**

Warm light. The table, the steam still rising off the rajma. Four empty chairs.
The match burns down. Goes out.

In the dark, very slowly, the room fades up in cold moonlight — the same room,
forty years dead. Roots in the floor. Rust on the plates. Your eye height is no
longer a child's. Nobody points this out.

Far away, through the walls, a sound like a drawer full of watches.

### Act I — Mummy (Kitchen)
- Find the old dining room in *Ab*; strike a match and watch *Tab* bloom out
  around the flame. Learn the rule by seeing it.
- First page of the diary is **not** here (see §6 — order matters).
- The kitchen: the floor is a pit in *Ab* and solid in *Tab*. Mummy's bangles
  sit on the shelf on the far side. Strike a match and walk across a floor that
  only exists while the flame does.
- **First sighting** (scripted, harmless, no sting): through the kitchen jaali,
  out in the moonlit courtyard, something very tall stands perfectly still,
  facing the house. When your next match flares, the window shows only the
  1987 courtyard — washing line, tulsi pot. When the match dies, it is still
  there. A little closer to the window. It does not come in. Not yet.
- Carry the bangles back before they crack in the cold *now*. Lay them at
  Mummy's plate.
- **Mummy returns** — a faceless woman in a maroon saree, seated, turned half
  toward the kitchen. In *Tab* she is solid. In *Ab* she is an outline of ash
  on the chair. Her bangles chime once.
- Then the house goes quiet. The crickets stop. The ticking is inside now.

### Act II — Dadi (Dadi's room)
- Every clock in the house is in here, hung on peepal roots. And in the
  corner, facing the wall, head bowed against the ceiling, **Kaal stands
  still.** Waiting the way Dadi said.
- Her spectacles are folded on the Gita on the pooja shelf — the shelf right
  beside it. The brass thali with the lamp oil and her cotton wicks is there too.
- The set piece: place candles to hold it, then walk past it, within reach,
  while its head turns to follow you. Take the spectacles and the thali. The
  candles burn down faster the closer they are to it.
- When you leave, it turns from the corner. It follows. It will always follow
  now.
- Lay the spectacles at Dadi's plate. **Dadi returns**: a small bent figure in
  a white shawl, the spectacles resting on a face that isn't there.

### Act III — Papa (Dining room)
- Papa's watch is on top of the TV in *Tab*; in *Ab* the TV is face-down under
  the roots. Take it in the light.
- It's stopped at 9:47. **Wind it.** It ticks. In the next room, every clock on
  every root answers at once — and Kaal knows exactly where the sound is.
- Lay the watch at Papa's plate. **Papa returns**, leaning back in his chair,
  checking a wrist that has nothing on it.
- The last page of the diary is under where the watch was (see §6). The player
  now knows who they are.
- Now light the family diya while Kaal walks toward the table: pour the oil,
  set the wick, strike, hold the flame to it. Every match you burn, it is a
  little closer.
- The diya catches. Warm light fills the whole room, and the whole room is
  1987. Kaal stops dead in the doorway — bent under the frame, ash hanging in
  the air around it. Its head turns toward the table.

### Finale — *The last dinner* (true ending)
See §7.

## 6. The diary pages (Munna's, written this week, found in *Ab*)

Three loose pages in an old man's hand. Optional to read, but placed on the
critical path so most players will. They are the slow reveal; order matters.

1. **Kitchen**, beside the dead stove:
   > Mummy's bangles. I always heard her before I saw her. At the end, in the
   > hospital, she kept asking for them and I couldn't find them. I still look.

2. **Dadi's room**, nailed among the clocks:
   > Dadi said Kaal takes the faces first. I laughed at her. I have every photo
   > of her that was ever taken, and I cannot remember her face.

3. **Dining room**, under the fallen TV, where Papa's watch was:
   > They are tearing the house down in the morning. The man from the Nigam
   > said I could take whatever I wanted. I didn't want anything. I wanted to
   > sit at that table one more time. The power's been cut for years. I brought
   > matches.

## 7. The endings

There are two. **In both, Kaal gets you.** Everything is temporary — winning
doesn't change that. What changes is what you had before it did.

### 7a. FAIL — *"Taken"* (caught in the dark)

Plays whenever Kaal reaches you while you're in darkness, or after your last
match goes out. First person, unbroken, no cuts, no sting — it is slow, and
the slowness is the horror. ~20 seconds.

| t (s) | What you see | What you hear |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | The match falls from your fingers. You watch it tumble and land on the floorboards, still burning, lighting the room from below. | Every sound in the house cuts out. Only the ticking, very close. |
| 1.5 | Two black hands come over your shoulders from behind — fingers far too long — and curl around your collarbones. They reach almost to your stomach. | Knuckles cracking, one by one, slowly. |
| 4.0 | You are lifted. The floor drops away. The burning match shrinks below you. The ceiling fan passes inches from your head. | Your breath, fast. Cloth tearing under the fingertips. |
| 6.5 | You are turned around in the air, slowly, to face it. The smooth blank head lowers until it fills the whole frame — pure black, nothing to look at, nothing looking back. It tilts. | **All the ticking stops at once.** Total silence. |
| 9.0 | Your view is forced down to your own hands, raised in front of you. They begin to age. Liver spots bloom. The skin thins to paper, then wrinkles, then **splits** across the knuckles. Blood wells up bright and goes dark and dries to black flakes in a heartbeat. The fingernails lift and fall away. | A wet crackle, then a dry one. Your heartbeat, slowing. |
| 12.5 | The flesh shrinks back from the fingers; the bones show through, yellow, then grey. A finger snaps and drops into the dark. The colour drains from the whole picture. A milky cataract haze creeps in from the edges of your vision. | A single bone snap. A thin, old man's breath. |
| 15.5 | One long black finger presses slowly into your chest. The shirt darkens, red spreading out from it. The hand closes. Your view tips back toward the ceiling. | A deep pressure sound; the heartbeat stops mid-beat. |
| 17.5 | The black of the head swallows the frame from the bottom up. | Silence. |
| 19.0 | **Grey morning.** The dining room from the doorway. A bulldozer idling outside. The burnt match on the floor. Your chair pulled out, empty. On the wall, the family photo: four blank figures — and where you stood, the paper has rotted through. | A diesel engine. Crows. |
| 23.0 | Card: **KAAL EATS EVERYONE.** | |

Options: **Try again** (retry from the last checkpoint) / **Title**.
On repeat deaths the sequence is cut to its last ~8 seconds and can be skipped
after two.

### 7b. TRUE ENDING — *"The Last Dinner"*

The diya is lit. The room is 1987. You sit in your chair. Across the table,
three faceless people eat dinner.

> **MUMMY:** What took you so long? The food's gone cold.
>
> **PAPA:** *(checks the watch on his wrist)* Quarter to ten.

The watch says 9:47. You watch the second hand move. 9:48. Time is moving again.

Mummy puts a roti on your plate. You reach for it — and in the full warm light,
for the first time, you see your hand clearly. Seventy years old. Liver-spotted.
A wedding ring worn thin. It trembles.

> **DADI:** Look how grown you are, Munna.

In the doorway, Kaal waits, folded under the frame. The oil in the diya is
going down.

Prompt: **blow out the diya** — or just wait. It burns down either way.

- **If you blow it out** (letting go): darkness at once. The ticking of Papa's
  watch keeps going. The long fingers come over your shoulders, gently this
  time. The aging happens to your hands on the tablecloth beside your plate —
  same as the fail death, the same gore — but your family is still sitting
  across from you in the last ember-glow, and they keep eating, and you are not
  alone for any of it. Morning: the bulldozer, the house coming down. In the
  rubble, the family photo — **four figures**, and the fourth is back.
  > **Everything lasts only a while. That's what made it precious.**

- **If you wait** (clinging): the flame shrinks, and the light shrinks with it,
  and the family goes first — Papa at the far end dissolving to ash, then Dadi,
  then Mummy reaching for you as her hand crumbles. You watch every one of them
  go. Then the flame goes. Then Kaal. Morning: the photo in the rubble has
  three figures. Your place in it is torn away.
  > **Kaal eats everyone.**

(If scope is tight, ship only *blow it out*: the prompt stays on screen and the
diya burns out into that same ending.)

## 8. Tone rules for writing

- All dialogue in English, subtitled. Short lines. Family names (Mummy,
  Papa, Dadi, Amma, Munna) stay as they are.
  Ordinary family talk. The horror is that it's ordinary.
- Nobody screams. Nobody explains Kaal except Dadi, once, at dinner.
- No jokes after the prologue. The prologue can be warm and a little funny
  (Papa and the TV, Dadi repeating herself); it has to be, so it hurts later.
- Kaal is never named on screen during play. Only in Dadi's story and on the
  title.
- Gore belongs only to Kaal's touch, and it is always *time* gore: aging,
  drying, decaying, breaking. Never cutting, never blood for its own sake.
